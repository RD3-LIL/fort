      Emberhold

      A Stronghold-style lordship: run the economy inside the castle, not just the walls around it.

      You start with a keep, 12 peasants, a granary and a few farms. Grow food, gather wood and stone, keep popularity above 60 so new families arrive, earn honour, claim the three estates, and be ready when raiders come. There is no final screen: see how grand a hold you can keep happy.

      New here? The guided tutorial teaches one mechanic at a time, in order, with the right buttons highlighted as you play. You can quit it any time and reopen it from the Tutorial button at the top.

      How to Play

      One mechanic at a time, in the order the game teaches them. Reopen this any time from the top of the screen.

      1. Gather wood and stone

      Everything you build costs wood, most strong buildings also cost stone. Woodcutters produce far more beside woods, hunters too. A quarry only works on open grass directly beside the grey stone outcrops (right side of the map). Build a Stockpile to store more of both.

      2. Build houses and farms

      Hovels come first, always: each beds 6 peasants, and new families only arrive if there is a spare bed. Every other building needs workers, and food jobs get staffed first. A Wheat Farm is the backbone; farms like open grass.

      3. Feed people, and vary the food

      Your people eat a little of whatever is in the granary every day. Four foods exist: bread, apples, cheese and meat. Variety is a popularity engine on its own: 2 food types gives +8, 3 gives +16, all 4 gives +24. One food every day wears folk down, and an empty granary is the fastest way to lose them.

      4. Set taxes and rations (Rule tab)

      Taxes turn people into gold but cost popularity, hard. Rations do the opposite trade: extra rations cost food and buy happiness, none at all is a disaster. Fair taxes and normal rations are the safe middle while you grow.

      5. Grow popularity, then population

      Popularity is recomputed daily and the Rule tab shows the full breakdown, line by line. At 60 or better, with a spare bed, new families arrive at the keep gate. Below 35, families leave in the night. Churches and inns each add +8.

      6. Earn honour (Feast tab)

      Honour is earned by good rule, not bought. A staffed church grants honour each month while popularity stays above 60, and contented folk add a little more. A Lord's Feast is the fast road: 8 of each food plus 30 gold buys +25 honour and a long happy glow.

      7. Claim an estate (Estates tab)

      Millbrook, Stonecroft and Riverford swear to your banner for honour, then send carts of food, wood, stone and gold every few days forever. Upgrade an estate with gold and its carts grow heavier.

      8. Handle crime (Rule tab)

      Crime climbs when folk are hungry, overtaxed or idle, and thieves steal gold and food in the night. Guard posts catch thieves and lower crime daily; the Stocks make an example of a caught thief and the village approves. Happy, busy, fed people barely steal at all.

      9. Defend the raid

      Scouts warn you about five days before raiders arrive. Defence is walls (+1), towers (+6), guard posts (+4) and knights (+8, recruited in the Rule tab for gold and honour). Beat the raid's strength and you loot their camp and gain honour; lose, and they take gold and food and shake the village for days.

      Controls

      Pick a building on the right, then click a grass tile to place it. Click any building on the map to see what it does. Pause, Play and Fast are under the map. Demolish mode pulls a building down with no refund.
